RESEARCH EXPERIENCE · 2026

Chung-Ang University.

Undergraduate research in RNA engineering and circRNA translation optimization at Kim Hakyun Lab, Chung-Ang University.

Research Focus

I worked on improving the translation efficiency of circular RNA, with a focus on IRES and UTR elements. The broader research objective is to contribute to a circular RNA-based vaccine platform that combines the durability of circRNA with improved protein expression.

Key Projects

PROJECT 01 · SZ1 IRES
Structural analysis of translation-enhancing mutations

I investigated why a single-nucleotide substitution in SZ1 IRES produced relatively high translation. By comparing predicted RNA secondary structures, I focused on a mutation that caused a distinctive local structural change near the end of a loop region.

This observation led me to consider whether changes in RNA structure or interactions with RNA-binding proteins could contribute to the translation phenotype. I proposed follow-up experiments to the professor and graduate researchers and carried out additional mutation and rescue experiments.

PROJECT 02 · IRES / UTR SCREENING
Systematic screening for high-efficiency combinations

I participated in a large-scale screening workflow involving approximately 90 IRES candidates. Higher-performing IRES candidates were selected for subsequent UTR testing, with approximately 50 UTR candidates evaluated in 5′, 3′, and combined configurations.

The screening was used to identify combinations associated with higher translation efficiency. Among the tested designs, EVB80 and FMDV IRES candidates showed promising activity, and an ACAT UTR combination at both the 5′ and 3′ positions produced a relatively high translation signal.

Experimental Approach

Construct design → PCR / cloning → plasmid preparation → transfection → luciferase assay → quantitative comparison → candidate selection

  • Molecular cloning including KLD and In-Fusion approaches
  • Primer redesign and overhang design for In-Fusion cloning
  • HEK293T cell culture and transient transfection
  • Luciferase-based evaluation of translation efficiency
  • IRES mutation and rescue experiments

Troubleshooting

Changing the experimental strategy

During IVT and circularization experiments, RNA bands were frequently degraded or difficult to visualize by PAGE. I considered RNA stability and circularization yield as possible causes and changed the strategy toward a Tornado-based DNA-level approach, allowing transcription and circularization to occur in mammalian cells.

I also compared the conventional KLD cloning workflow with In-Fusion cloning. This required adapting primer design and incorporating vector-compatible overhangs. These changes made the workflow more practical and produced more stable experimental results.

What I Learned

These experiments taught me to treat unexpected results as questions rather than simply as failed experiments. In particular, the SZ1 IRES work gave me experience connecting an observed phenotype to RNA structure, forming a hypothesis, proposing a follow-up experiment, and testing that hypothesis experimentally.
